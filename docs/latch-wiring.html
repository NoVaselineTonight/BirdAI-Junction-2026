Latch Wiring

This connects the two latch servos to the Arduino UNO Q. Unplug the board and the servo supply from power before you wire anything.

Servo A = servo1 in the sketch, on pin D9. Servo B = servo2, on pin D10. Servo B is mounted mirrored.

Recommended: separate 5 V supply

Finding the pins

Hold the board with USB-C on your left. The long header along the top edge has a small gap in it.

From that gap, walk left toward USB-C: D8, then D9, then D10. Keep going: D11, D12, D13, then GND.

If the labels printed on your board disagree with this, stop and trust the board. Ask before wiring.

Connection table

Servo plugs are female, so use male-to-male jumper wires from the plug to the pins. Wire colours below are the servo's own wires. DFRobot's own page could not be opened. A third-party page for the same DF9GMS 180° servo gives red = power, brown = ground, orange = signal. It also mentions a variant with gray and yellow wires. If your wires are gray/red/yellow: gray = ground, red = power, yellow = signal.

Not sure which wire is which? On a standard servo plug the middle wire is power (+). Of the two outer wires, the darkest one is ground. The remaining one is signal.

Do not

  • Never put 5 V on D3 or A0–A5. These pins only take up to about 3.6 V. 5 V there can destroy the microcontroller. They are ringed in red in the drawings.
  • Never put a red (power) wire on a signal pin such as D9 or D10. Signal pins carry a 3.3 V control pulse, not motor power.
  • Never swap a servo's red and brown wires. Power the wrong way round can kill the servo. And never wire with anything plugged in: one slipped jumper can short the supply.

Fallback: no separate supply Riskier

Use this only if you have no separate 5 V supply. Both servos take power from the UNO Q's own 5V pin. The signal wires stay the same: D9 and D10. No extra ground jumper is needed, because the servos already share the board's ground.

The risk. A servo pulls a burst of current every time it starts to move. That burst can drag the board's 5 V down far enough to reset the UNO Q, which is also running the demo.

What lowers the risk:

  • Power the board from a strong USB-C supply: 5 V, 3 A, with a good cable.
  • Fit the capacitor shown above, right at the servo power wires. Its striped side must go to GND. Backwards, it can burst.
  • Move one servo at a time. Note: the current sketch moves both at the same moment.
  • Make sure nothing jams the flap. A stalled servo keeps pulling its biggest current.

No stall-current figure for the SER0006 was found, so we cannot say how big that burst is.

What the datasheet says about the 5V pin. The 5V pin is wired straight to the board's internal 5 V line, which USB-C feeds through a diode. So with only USB-C plugged in, the pin does give out 5 V. The datasheet gives no current limit for taking power out of this pin. Its "up to 3 A" figure is for feeding power into the board through the pin. The board, its Wi-Fi and both servos all share whatever the USB-C supply gives, at most 3 A. Because of the diode, the pin will sit a little below 5 V Not confirmed how much

Check before power

  1. Board USB-C and servo supply are both unplugged while you check.
  2. Orange wires sit on D9 (servo A) and D10 (servo B), and nothing touches D3 or A0–A5.
  3. Every red wire goes to +5 V and every brown wire goes to GND. No red wire is on a D pin.
  4. Separate supply: the black jumper joins UNO Q GND to supply GND.
  5. No bare metal of two wires touches, and the flap can swing freely by hand.

First power-on

When the latch sketch starts, both servos swing to the open position and stay there. They may move fast, so keep fingers out of the flap. Servo B turns the opposite way to A, because it is mounted mirrored.

Until the sketch is uploaded, the servos do nothing at all. That is normal, not a wiring fault. Linux on the board also takes a while to start; the LED matrix shows a logo for about 20–30 seconds meanwhile.

Not confirmed

  • Servo wire colours (brown = ground, red = +5 V, orange = signal) are confirmed by a third-party page only; DFRobot's own page could not be opened. Gray/red/yellow variant: gray = ground, red = power, yellow = signal. Check the plug: the middle wire is power.
  • 3.3 V signal is enough for the SER0006 is assumed from typical hobby servos. No source states the signal level.
  • Servo current. Rated voltage is 4.8 V per a distributor listing. No stall-current figure was found.
  • Which pins cannot take 5 V. The official pinout sheet's warning names only A0 and A1. The datasheet text is stricter: also D3, and A0–A5 when used as analog pins. This page keeps the stricter rule.
  • Printed labels on the board were not checked. Pin order comes from the official UNO Q datasheet pinout. If your board's printing disagrees, trust the board and ask.
  • How much current the 5V pin can give when the board runs on USB-C. The datasheet does not say.
  • The 5V pin's exact voltage on USB-C. It sits a bit below 5 V because of a diode. The SER0006 is rated 4.8 V (distributor listing), so it may run slightly under its rating.
  • Capacitor size and voltage (470–1000 µF, 10 V or more) and the claim that reversed servo power kills a servo are common practice, not from a source checked here.
