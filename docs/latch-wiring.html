Latch Wiring

This connects the latch servo to the Arduino UNO Q, powered from the board itself. Unplug the USB-C cable from the board before you wire anything.

One servo, servo in the sketch, on pin D9. It turns one axis, and both pull wires of the latch are fixed to that axis.

Wiring: servo powered from the board

Finding the pins

Hold the board with USB-C on your left.

Top edge (signals): the long header has a small gap in it. From that gap, walk left toward USB-C: D8, then D9.

Bottom edge (power): the shorter header starts at the USB-C end: BOOT, IOREF, RESET, 3V3, then 5V (5th pin), then GND, GND, VIN.

If the labels printed on your board disagree with this, stop and trust the board. Ask before wiring.

Connection table

Servo plugs are female, so use male-to-male jumper wires from the plug to the pins. Wire colours are the servo's own wires. DFRobot's own page could not be opened. A third-party page for the same DF9GMS 180° servo gives red = power, brown = ground, orange = signal. It also mentions a variant with gray and yellow wires. If your wires are gray/red/yellow: gray = ground, red = power, yellow = signal.

No breadboard is needed. One servo means one red wire on 5V and one brown wire on GND. Each header pin holds one jumper, and nothing has to be joined.

Not sure which wire is which? On a standard servo plug the middle wire is power (+). Of the two outer wires, the darkest one is ground. The remaining one is signal.

The risk. A servo pulls a burst of current every time it starts to move. Here that burst comes from the board's own 5 V. It can drag the 5 V down far enough to reset the UNO Q, which is also running the demo. No stall-current figure for the SER0006 was found, so we cannot say how big that burst is.

What lowers the risk:

  • Keep the flap light, so the servo barely works to move it.
  • Make sure nothing can jam the flap. A stalled servo keeps pulling its biggest current.
  • Give the board as much USB-C power as you can. The datasheet asks for a 5 V / 3 A supply.

If the board resets when the servo moves, use Setup 2 below or the separate 5 V supply wiring.

What the datasheet says about the 5V pin. The 5V pin is wired straight to the board's internal 5 V line, which USB-C feeds through a diode. So with only USB-C plugged in, the pin does give out 5 V. The datasheet gives no current limit for taking power out of this pin. Its "up to 3 A" figure is for feeding power into the board through the pin. The board, its Wi-Fi and the servo all share whatever the USB-C side gives, at most 3 A. Because of the diode, the pin will sit a little below 5 V Not confirmed how much

Mechanics

  • The servo is a 180 degree position servo. It does not spin round and round. It turns at most half a turn.
  • How far it pulls. Put a spool or horn of radius r on the axis. Half a turn pulls about 3.1 × r of wire (π × r). The sketch's default travel, 10° to 100°, is a quarter turn and pulls about 1.6 × r. Example: r = 1 cm gives about 1.6 cm of wire at the default angles, and up to about 3 cm if you widen the angles to 0 and 180.
  • How hard it pulls. Rated torque is about 1.6 kg·cm at 4.8 V (DigiKey lists 156.9 mNm for the SER0006). Divide by r to get the pull: r = 1 cm gives about 1.6 kg at stall. Plan for well under half of that for both wires together: about 0.5 kg at r = 1 cm, about 0.25 kg at r = 2 cm. A servo that is held stalled gets hot and pulls enough current to reset the board.
  • Both wires go on the same axis, wound the same way, so they rise together.
  • The open and closed angles are the two constants at the top of the sketch (SERVO_OPEN and SERVO_CLOSED). Adjust them to the real latch.

Do not

  • Never put 5 V on D3 or A0–A5. These pins only take up to about 3.6 V. 5 V there can destroy the microcontroller. They are ringed in red in the drawings.
  • Never put a red (power) wire on a signal pin such as D9, and never on 3V3 or VIN. Red goes to 5V only.
  • Never swap a servo's red and brown wires. Power the wrong way round can kill the servo. And never wire with the USB-C cable plugged in: one slipped jumper can short the board's 5 V.

Better, if you find a 5 V supply

A separate 5 V supply for the servo (for example a power bank with a cut USB cable or a USB breakout) takes the servo bursts off the board. The signal stays on D9. The red and brown wires move to the supply, and one black jumper joins the board's GND to the supply's GND. Nothing goes to the board's 5V pin.

Check before power

  1. The USB-C cable is unplugged from the board while you check.
  2. The orange wire sits on D9, and nothing touches D3 or A0–A5.
  3. The red wire reaches 5V on the bottom edge, and no red wire is on a D pin, 3V3 or VIN.
  4. The brown wire sits on the GND pin right after 5V, and no bare metal of two wires touches.
  5. The flap swings freely by hand.

First power-on

When the latch sketch starts, the servo swings to the open position and stays there. It may move fast, so keep fingers out of the flap.

Until the sketch is uploaded, the servo does nothing at all. That is normal, not a wiring fault. Linux on the board also takes a while to start; the LED matrix shows a logo for about 20–30 seconds meanwhile.

Not confirmed

  • Servo wire colours (brown = ground, red = +5 V, orange = signal) are confirmed by a third-party page only; DFRobot's own page could not be opened. Gray/red/yellow variant: gray = ground, red = power, yellow = signal. Check the plug: the middle wire is power.
  • 3.3 V signal is enough for the SER0006 is assumed from typical hobby servos. No source states the signal level.
  • Servo current. Rated voltage is 4.8 V per a distributor listing. No stall-current figure was found.
  • How much current the 5V pin can give when the board runs on USB-C. The datasheet does not say.
  • The 5V pin's exact voltage on USB-C. It sits a bit below 5 V because of a diode, so the servo may run slightly under its 4.8 V rating.
  • How much current the laptop's USB-C port gives. The datasheet asks for a 5 V / 3 A supply. Many laptop ports give less.
  • Whether this hub has a power-in port, and whether it passes 5 V / 3 A through to the board. Nobody has checked the hub yet.
  • Whether this camera works on the UNO Q. The datasheet lists a USB camera as a supported kind of device through a powered dongle; this camera has not been tried.
  • Which pins cannot take 5 V. The official pinout sheet's warning names only A0 and A1. The datasheet text is stricter: also D3, and A0–A5 when used as analog pins. This page keeps the stricter rule.
  • Printed labels on the board were not checked. Pin order comes from the official UNO Q datasheet pinout. If your board's printing disagrees, trust the board and ask.
  • The claim that reversed servo power kills a servo is common practice, not from a source checked here.
